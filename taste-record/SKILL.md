# Taste record

Agents tend to ship the defaults their models reach for. A person rejects
one, the next session makes it again, because nothing it loads remembers the
rejection. A taste record is the memory: reactions written down as they
happen, and rules the owner has declared from them.

## Before design work

1. Read the record's rules and open tensions before you draw or write
   anything the owner will see.
2. For every visible element, ask "chosen or defaulted?". If nothing in the
   record or the brief chose it, say so, and replace it or ask.
3. When you hand design work to a sub-agent, paste the hard rejections into
   its brief and tell it to read the record. A sub-agent has not loaded
   what you loaded, and will reach for the same defaults.

## When the owner reacts

1. Append one dated observation: the date, what was on screen, and the
   owner's exact words in quotation marks. Do not paraphrase, and do not
   generalise to a rule.
2. Write it where it belongs. Keep three places apart: the owner's own taste
   across projects, rules a project has decided, and raw reactions in a
   project. A reaction to one screen is not yet personal taste.

## Rules

1. Never promote an observation to a rule yourself, however often it recurs.
   You may propose one, citing the observations behind it.
2. A rule is locked only when the owner declares it. Record the declaration:
   who, when, and their words.
3. Write each rule as "never X; instead, Y". A ban with no replacement sends
   the next agent to its next default.
4. Say where each rule is enforced (a lint rule, a test, a checklist), or
   write "not yet enforced". Most rules start there; say so rather than imply
   a check exists.

## Tensions

When two things the owner wants pull against each other, log the tension
with its date and leave it open. Do not resolve it, and do not pick a side
quietly in the work. Only the owner closes one.

## What it does not do

It does not make the work look like anyone in particular. A list of things
to avoid only removes the commonest defaults, and a look the owner once
chose can become a default itself. Only specifics (named references
such as particular sites, books or designers, declared tensions, one
deliberate signature) make it theirs. Nor does
writing a rule down mean anything reads it: if the record is loaded by an
instruction rather than a check, an agent can still skip it.
